    Payroll Calculation FAQ in Omne RH Payroll¶

    Answers to common questions about how the payroll calculation engine works.

    What is the difference between a monthly payroll and an extra pay?¶

    A monthly payroll (type "Payroll") calculates the current month earnings: base salary, supplements, overtime, employee SS deductions, and income tax withholding.

    An extra pay (type "Extra Pay") calculates the summer or Christmas bonus when these are not prorated monthly. If extra payments are prorated in 12 monthly installments, they are automatically included as a proration line in each monthly payroll and do not need a separate calculation.

    What happens if an employee has two active contracts in the same period?¶

    The engine processes each contract independently within the same calculation. If an employee has two active contracts (for example, one ending on day 15 and another starting on day 16), the engine:

    1. Calculates the payroll for the first contract proportionally to its days (1-15).
    2. Calculates the payroll for the second contract proportionally to its days (16-30).

    Each segment can have a different perception scheme if the agreement or category changed.

    Why is net pay different from gross minus tax minus social security?¶

    In addition to the employee SS contribution and income tax withholding, the engine may automatically deduct:

    • Advances pending from previous payrolls
    • Automatic deductions: loans, court garnishments, payments on account
    • Pension plan contributions: employee contributions
    • Benefits in kind: withholding on account that reduces net pay

    All these concepts appear as "Deduction" type lines in the payroll detail.

    What is the difference between back pay and salary differences?¶

    • Back pay (atrasos): Calculated when a new labor agreement is approved with retroactive effects. The engine computes differences between amounts already paid and new amounts. They are declared with the "Back Pay" fiscal marker and the accrual year.
    • Differences: Calculated for one-off errors requiring adjustment (for example, a retroactive category change). Unlike back pay, differences may affect individual concepts only.

    The fiscal distinction matters: back pay has special income tax treatment (attributed to the accrual year), while differences are attributed to the current year.

    What is the difference between a standard complementary payroll and batch complementary payrolls?¶

    A standard complementary payroll (back pay) is calculated from a perception scheme that you configure manually. It produces one complete payroll, with earnings, deductions, contributions, and income tax, for a single period.

    Batch complementary payrolls (Generate complementary payrolls in batch) are a different, automated process:

    • They start from an extra pay period to define the contribution periods.
    • They automatically generate the perception scheme lines, prorated month by month.
    • They calculate a complementary payroll for each month, keeping only social security contribution bases (no earnings or income tax lines).
    • They move the employee's contribution to the settlement month's monthly payroll as a single deduction line.

    Use batch complementary payrolls when you need to regularize contribution bases for several months retroactively, such as after a change to which salary concepts are subject to contribution for extra pay.

    How does net-to-gross calculation work?¶

    When a concept has the CALCNETO calculation key, the engine performs an inverse calculation:

    1. Calculates the payroll normally to determine SS and income tax deductions.
    2. Adjusts the gross amount so that after deductions, the net result matches the specified amount exactly.
    3. This adjustment only works with a single CALCNETO concept and no sick leave incidents in the period.

    If conditions are not met, the engine disables the adjustment and calculates normally.

    What happens if I forget to register an incident before calculating?¶

    The engine assumes the employee worked the entire period. This causes overstated earnings and incorrect contribution bases. The solution is:

    1. Void the calculated payroll.
    2. Register the incident with correct dates.
    3. Recalculate the payroll.

    It is not possible to add an incident to an already calculated payroll without voiding it first.

    Can I calculate multiple months at once?¶

    No. The engine calculates one period at a time. Each month may depend on the previous month's results (such as IT contribution bases). Calculate each month sequentially.

    What is the difference between Samples mode and a real calculation?¶

    Samples (simulation) mode calculates all amounts identically to a real calculation but does not create employee ledger entries. It can be overwritten without voiding. Use it to verify results before committing to a definitive calculation.
